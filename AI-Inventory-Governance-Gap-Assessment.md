# AI Inventory, Risk Categorization, and Governance Gap Assessment

## Project Overview

As organizations increasingly adopt Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools, maintaining visibility into AI usage and associated risks becomes essential.

This project demonstrates how to establish an AI inventory, assess risks, evaluate governance controls, and identify governance gaps using internationally recognized AI governance frameworks. The goal is to create a practical approach for understanding and governing AI systems used within an organization.

## Project Objectives

- Identify AI systems currently in use
- Categorize AI systems based on risk
- Assess governance and oversight mechanisms
- Identify governance gaps
- Recommend improvement actions
- Align findings with regulatory and industry frameworks

## Frameworks Used

**EU AI Act** — Risk-based approach · High-risk AI classification · Transparency requirements · Human oversight

**ISO/IEC 42001** — AI Management System (AIMS) · AI risk assessment · AI lifecycle governance

**NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF)** — Govern · Map · Measure · Manage

**UK AI Regulation Principles** — Safety · Transparency · Fairness · Accountability · Contestability

**OECD AI Principles** — Inclusive growth · Human-centered values · Transparency · Robustness · Accountability

## Phase 2: Risk Categorization

Assess each AI system using a simple risk model.

> **Key question:** What is the worst-case scenario if the system fails?

| Risk Level | Example Categories |
|---|---|
| **Low** | Content drafting, grammar correction, meeting summaries |
| **Medium** | Internal analytics, productivity tools, customer support assistants |
| **High** | Recruitment, healthcare, credit decisions, education assessments |

## Phase 3: Governance Assessment

Review existing governance controls against five question areas.

| Area | Governance Questions |
|---|---|
| **Human Oversight** | Is a human reviewing outputs? Can decisions be overridden? |
| **Transparency** | Is the purpose clearly explained? Are limitations documented? |
| **Privacy** | Is personal data protected? Is there a privacy policy? |
| **Security** | Are access controls implemented? Are logs maintained? |
| **Accountability** | Who owns the system? Who manages incidents? |

## Phase 4: Governance Gap Analysis

Compare current practices against governance requirements.

### Example Assessment

| Requirement | Status | Gap Identified |
|---|---|---|
| Human Oversight | Partial | Manual review inconsistent |
| Transparency | No | AI decisions not explained |
| Privacy Controls | Yes | No major gaps |
| Logging | Partial | User activity not monitored |
| Accountability | No | Owner not formally assigned |

## Phase 5: Recommendations

**Governance**
- Establish AI governance roles
- Create AI acceptable use policies
- Maintain an AI inventory

**Risk Management**
- Implement an AI risk register
- Conduct AI impact assessments
- Define escalation procedures

**Security**
- Classify AI-related data
- Enable logging and monitoring
- Review access permissions regularly

**Compliance**
- Align with EU AI Act requirements
- Conduct annual governance reviews
- Maintain audit evidence

## Deliverables

1. **AI Inventory Register** — Document all AI tools currently in use.
2. **AI Risk Register** — Track identified risks and mitigation measures.
3. **Governance Gap Assessment Report** — Identify strengths, weaknesses, and priorities.
4. **Executive Summary Dashboard** — Provide a management-friendly overview of findings and recommendations.
